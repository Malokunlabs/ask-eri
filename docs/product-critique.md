# Product critique: make ERI useful for a real market decision

Reviewed 20 October 2026 against the current application, published aggregate snapshot and field-brief workflow. Recommendations below are product hypotheses; no buyer interviews, willingness-to-pay results or revenue are established.

## Product judgment

ERI has a credible foundation for a field-intelligence service. The website currently serves a curious reader better than a recurring decision maker. It exposes six supported findings and transparent limitations, but asks the visitor to supply much of the interpretation, choose a generic calculator and initiate research without seeing an example deliverable.

The next milestone should be a buyer saying, “This helped me choose what to test or verify, and the follow-up was worth paying for.” A larger feature count or a moving chart cannot establish that.

Proposed promise: **Understand how businesses in this market operate, identify what your decision still needs, and commission a focused return to the field.** Keep the African ambition, with the current Yaba–Sabo–Tejuosho evidence boundary visible at the point of use.

## Current evidence and what it can support

The published snapshot contains 115 initial observations dated 7–16 October 2026. All identities are provisional; 27 included observations carry unresolved identity-review flags, and one shifted row was recovered from actual source answers. They are observations, not 115 verified unique businesses. There are no reviewed matched longitudinal comparisons, representative market estimates or calibrated outcome predictions.

| Supported finding | Useful decision question | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 85/104 classifiable responses report higher restocking expenditure | What price, quantity, supplier or product-mix changes should a distributor investigate? | Inflation, optimal order size, profit, stockout probability |
| 56/115 report regular or occasional remote orders | Is a voluntary phone/message ordering pilot worth investigating? | Channel revenue, willingness to adopt, likely conversion |
| 70/114 name transfer as primary payment | Which payment/reconciliation practices should a pilot verify? | Transaction market share, settlement reliability, which remote-order respondents use transfers |
| 56/112 select no records | How should a pilot document activity without assuming written records exist? | Poor management, uncreditworthiness, individual overlap with other findings |
| 30/115 report borrowing or goods on credit | What forms of supplier credit or borrowing should follow-up distinguish? | Unmet finance demand, eligibility, repayment capacity or default risk |
| 89/114 recall better business conditions | What does “better” mean, and does it persist on a return visit? | Measured growth, causal impact, sales or profit change |

The strongest near-term use is to choose a narrower investigation. These separate totals must not be fused into a fictional merchant profile or a claim that one characteristic causes another. Including previously held rows expanded the cohort under an approved policy; it did not prove economic improvement.

## Who should come first

| Audience | Current value | Main shortfall | Priority hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distributor/procurement/route-to-market teams | Restocking and remote-ordering findings can inform a concrete field question | No product-level price/quantity, supplier, replenishment or verified outlet coverage | Primary buyer discovery and first scoped paid pilot |
| Financial-institution pilot/product teams | Payment, records and existing-credit questions can shape voluntary discovery | Calculator is budget arithmetic, with no suitability, affordability or demand evidence | Secondary path; research planning only |
| Research/development partners | Transparent baseline and explicit gaps can support a scoped research discussion | No verified panel, sampling frame, instrument-comparability or attrition record | Secondary buyer; potential sponsor of panel development |
| Participating merchants | Their observations underpin the product | No clear return of value or practical participation workflow yet | Essential participant constituency; do not assume they are the first paying audience |
| General public/media | Accessible local facts and explanations | Broad economic framing can exceed the evidence; no sustained editorial output yet | Public readership, not the first revenue test |

Distributors are the first hypothesis because their decisions align most closely with the existing restocking evidence, not because their demand or budgets have been verified. Access matters: an available research sponsor with a real, funded decision could validate the service sooner. Preserve the other explorers, but give the homepage one clear primary journey.

## What is working

- Dates, denominators, refusals and QA warnings make the evidence inspectable. Honest limits can become a reason to trust ERI.
- A quote-first field brief is a plausible first service. It has a clear customer, question, scope and deliverable without pretending that a form submission is a sale.
- The public baseline can attract interest while a scoped return visit addresses the customer's actual uncertainty.
- The longitudinal gate correctly leaves future points unplotted. It makes the missing evidence explicit.
- Topic opt-in and confirmation separate a requested brief from ongoing updates.

## What I would challenge

### 1. The front door promises more than the answer engine provides

“Ask the real economy” and an unrestricted question box suggest broad conversational coverage. The implementation routes conservatively to six fixed evidence topics. Repeated unsupported answers may feel like product failure despite being correct refusals. Show supported questions and scope before typing; offer a deliberate “help ERI investigate this” action when evidence is missing. Do not silently retain arbitrary questions.

### 2. There is too much interpretation work for the buyer

Counts answer “what was reported,” but not “why is this worth my next 15 minutes?” Add one short, editorially reviewed decision brief near the top: observation → possible relevance → unknown → next field question. Keep possible relevance explicitly an interpretation. Move technical QA into expandable detail while retaining the material identity/coverage warning beside the finding.

### 3. The calculators can be useful without being the product

Distributor arithmetic makes a trade-off visible. Finance pilot multiplication and research visit budgeting are easy to reproduce elsewhere. Treat them as aids for specifying a question, and judge them by qualified enquiries and completed studies. Avoid presenting generic arithmetic as a data-derived recommendation or differentiator.

### 4. The site is a long browse before it is a clear journey

Coverage, six signals, analysis, method, three audience paths, graph and two contact forms compete for attention. Recommended entry journey: current market brief → inspect evidence → explore a decision → request a scoped investigation. Keep other material accessible, with fewer equal-weight calls to action.

### 5. A subscription needs an editorial reason to return

Daily processing is infrastructure cadence, not a promise of new daily intelligence. Send a useful issue only when evidence, interpretation or a reviewed study warrants it. Distinguish new observations, method/QA revisions, matched changes and product announcements. A methodology change should never look like a market alert.

### 6. The longitudinal asset is a field operation

Showing more graphs cannot create a panel. Resolve identities, document return-visit consent and question meaning, define visit windows, track refusals and loss to follow-up, and return to the same businesses. Establish a sampling approach appropriate to the claim before expanding geographic or sector comparisons. The existing ten-pair threshold is a reporting floor, not proof of precision or representativeness.

### 7. Paid fulfillment needs an owner and a visible output

A working confirmation queue does not provide scoping, field capacity, participant consent, analysis, editorial QA, delivery or customer follow-up. Publish an honest sample brief now; appoint owners and capture customer approval outside the site before commissioning work. Live email/storage, sender identity, deployed delivery tests and Vercel authorization are still activation gates.

### 8. Participation should return value

A future merchant participation journey should explain the question, effort, optional contact/revisit consent, privacy choices and what the participant receives. Start with a plain-language summary of their own reported answers or an approved aggregate market summary. Do not promise financing, commercial leads or benefits not arranged. Buyer notification consent is not merchant research consent.

## Recommended build sequence

The detailed implementation contract and a sample brief are in `product-next-sprint.md`.

1. **Build now from the existing aggregates:** a dated market brief, clear supported-question entry points, printable/citable evidence cards and a sample scoped deliverable. This improves usefulness without pretending to have a new dataset.
2. **In parallel, validate the buyer:** speak with 5–10 reachable decision makers about a recent real decision; seek one approved, scoped paid field brief. These are proposed research targets, not completed interviews or conversion forecasts. Do not contact anyone without explicit authorization.
3. **Make one service work end to end:** activate and verify email/storage, establish intake/scoping/editorial owners, record quote approval, deliver an agreed brief, ask what decision it changed and calculate fulfillment cost. No paid subscription or automated checkout promise until delivery is repeatable.
4. **Build the evidence asset:** reconcile the 27 review flags and consent a feasible return-visit cohort. Select actual measures from the funded question. For restocking, agree comparable units/products and separate price from quantity and mix; for ordering, record actual use rather than assume adoption. Do not select a panel size without considering consent, effort, attrition and analysis needs.
5. **Expand only after review:** publish matched comparisons when methods permit; consider an ongoing market watch only if repeat customers value the cadence. Add another location after its geography, identities and collection method are explicit. Add richer within-cohort comparisons only after approved source fields, disclosure review and denominator rules exist.

Delay more audience calculators, national maps, predictive scores, merchant credit rankings, automated prices and a general-purpose AI answer layer. Each adds product or credibility risk before proving the first decision-to-fieldwork loop.

## Revenue and learning

Keep the public baseline and basic exploration accessible. Test scoped field briefs first: agree question, geography, measures, deliverable, price, timing and permitted use. Only then consider a funded repeat panel or recurring evidence watch. Treat those as distinct services, not automatically three subscription tiers. Set no prices or turnaround guarantees until delivery cost and customer value are known. Customers fund questions and collection, not preferred findings or access to private merchant records.

Measure: qualified decision questions → scoping conversations → quotes → customer approvals → paid work → reviewed delivery → reported decision usefulness → repeat purchase. Record effort and gross margin per brief alongside conversion. For the evidence asset, track identity resolution, consented revisits, complete pairs per measure, attrition and QA revisions. For updates, track confirmed useful-topic interest, replies, delivery failures and removals. Raw visits, email addresses and added source rows are insufficient success measures.

## Useful external benchmarks

The World Bank's [Enterprise Surveys methodology](https://www.enterprisesurveys.org/en/methodology) describes panel data as interviews with the same firms across years and distinguishes sampling methods and comparable instruments. The relevant lesson for ERI is continuity and comparability; this is not a claim that ERI uses or meets that methodology.

Our World in Data's [reuse and citation guidance](https://ourworldindata.org/faqs) makes charts downloadable and asks users to credit both its work and underlying providers. ERI can apply that product lesson to aggregate evidence cards: useful outside the website, with provenance and qualifications traveling with them. It is not permission to publish ERI's private row-level source.

## Decision for this build cycle

Prioritize the dated, distributor-led market brief and example field deliverable before another calculator. Then validate one paid decision and one feasible repeat-visit protocol. The possible long-term advantage is a trusted, consented memory of the same businesses over time, connected to decisions people actually make.
